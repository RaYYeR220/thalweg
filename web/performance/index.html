Performance · COOL on Graviton4

An honest negative result.

One complete gauging of an 8-second clip (VGC video 1, deterministic agent), five times on each build: COOL's OpenCV 5.1 on Graviton4, the stock OpenCV 5 wheel on the same Graviton4 instance, and the same wheel on x86. All three reach the same decision; the two Arm builds agree on the discharge to four decimals.

Finding one

COOL ≈ the stock wheel.

38.9 s against 37.7 s

On this workload COOL is not faster than opencv-python-headless on the same c8g.large. The aarch64 wheel already ships KleidiCV, in a newer version than the COOL build, and our time goes into ensemble correlation, ALIKED/LightGlue inference and numpy, not the colour, resize and contour kernels COOL targets.

Finding two

Graviton4 is the saving.

$0.84 against $0.98

Per 1000 gaugings with the same wheel, Graviton4 costs about 15 % less than x86 (c7i.large). COOL's AMI adds a software fee, which brings its cost to $0.97, close to x86.

What runs in production

The worker runs COOL.

c8g.large

The production worker uses COOL's build, and each benchmark file records which cv2 ran, its build HAL, the instance type and the AMI product code; they are reproduced below. The obvious next step is our own kernels: LSPIV and STIV are Python loops around OpenCV calls and are the two largest stages.
